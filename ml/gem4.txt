### p16
Academic dishonesty among students often stems from intense pressure to achieve high grades, overwhelming workloads, and a fear of failure. When grades determine scholarships, university admissions, or career paths, students may prioritize the final score over actual learning. Additionally, poor time management and lack of preparation can lead to desperation, driving students to seek shortcuts during exams or assignments. In high school, I once saw a friend cheat on an algebra test simply because he was terrified of losing his spot on the basketball team due to a single bad grade. He knew the material was important, but the immediate threat of failing overwhelmed his judgment.

To discourage cheating, schools must address both the environment and the incentives that drive it. Institutions can reduce academic pressure by designing diverse assessment methods, such as project-based evaluations and open-book exams, which emphasize critical thinking rather than simple memorization. Educators should also cultivate a culture where asking for help is normalized and academic integrity is actively discussed. Furthermore, implementing clear consequences alongside robust student support services—like tutoring centers and time-management workshops—helps students build confidence in their own abilities. When schools create an environment that values effort, personal growth, and understanding over perfection, students are far less likely to resort to cheating.

### p17
An effective and memorable teacher possesses a rare blend of deep subject knowledge, genuine empathy, and an ability to connect with students on a personal level. Rather than simply delivering lectures, great educators foster an inclusive environment where questions are welcomed and curiosity is actively encouraged. They adapt their teaching methods to accommodate different learning styles, ensuring that no student feels left behind. During my junior year, my chemistry teacher noticed I was struggling with complex reactions and took extra time after class to explain the concepts using simple real-world analogies. That small gesture not only saved my grade but also completely restored my confidence in the subject.

Beyond academic instruction, memorable teachers inspire long-term personal growth by demonstrating patience and enthusiasm. They recognize their students' potential even when the students themselves cannot see it, offering encouragement that extends far beyond the classroom walls. By modeling integrity, passion, and kindness, these educators leave a lasting imprint on their students' lives. Ultimately, the most impactful teachers are not remembered merely for the facts they taught, but for how they made their students feel valued, capable, and eager to learn.

### p18
Excessive screen time poses significant risks to physical health, mental well-being, and social development. Prolonged exposure to digital devices frequently leads to eye strain, poor posture, and disrupted sleep patterns due to the blue light emitted by screens. On a psychological level, spending hours on phones or computers often correlates with increased rates of anxiety, reduced attention spans, and social isolation. Last semester, I spent nearly six hours a day scrolling through my phone during finals week, which left me feeling constantly exhausted, unfocused, and unable to sleep properly until I deliberately set screen limits.

To mitigate these harmful effects, individuals must establish healthy boundaries with technology. Balancing digital engagement with physical exercise, outdoor activities, and face-to-face interactions is essential for maintaining overall wellness. While technology offers undeniable benefits for learning and communication, moderation is critical. Recognizing the early signs of screen fatigue and intentionally scheduling tech-free breaks throughout the day helps protect both physical health and mental clarity, ensuring that digital tools serve to enhance life rather than dominate it.

### p19
Automation and artificial intelligence will dramatically reshape the job market over the next two years, eliminating routine manual and administrative tasks while creating new demands for specialized skills. Jobs involving repetitive data entry, basic manufacturing, and routine customer service are increasingly being replaced by automated systems. However, this transformation also generates new opportunities in technical fields such as software engineering, data analysis, and robotics maintenance. When I worked a summer job at a retail warehouse, I watched automated sorting machines replace several manual packing stations, but the company simultaneously hired technicians to program and maintain those new machines.

As a result, future employment will heavily prioritize uniquely human capabilities that machines cannot easily replicate. Skills such as complex problem-solving, emotional intelligence, adaptability, and creative innovation will become essential across virtually every industry. Educational institutions and workers must focus on lifelong learning to adapt to these shifting demands. While automation creates temporary disruption and job displacement, it ultimately shifts the workforce toward more engaging, strategic, and human-centric roles, making adaptability the single most valuable asset for future workers.

### p20
Efficient public transportation plays a vital role in improving urban quality of life by reducing traffic congestion, cutting air pollution, and increasing mobility for all residents. In densely populated cities, heavy gridlock wastes time, lowers economic productivity, and increases environmental harm. High-capacity transit systems, such as metros, light rails, and dedicated bus lines, move thousands of people efficiently while using a fraction of the road space required by private vehicles. When I visited a major metropolitan area last year, taking the city subway allowed me to cross town in fifteen minutes, whereas sitting in a taxi along the same route took nearly an hour in peak traffic.

Beyond saving time, public transit makes cities more equitable and accessible. Affordable fares ensure that low-income workers, students, and elderly individuals can reach jobs, schools, and healthcare facilities without the burden of car ownership costs. Investing in reliable, safe public transportation also frees up urban space previously dedicated to parking lots, allowing cities to create green parks and walkable pedestrian areas. Ultimately, robust transit networks make crowded urban environments far more livable, sustainable, and connected.
