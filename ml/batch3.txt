### p11

Friendship is an important part of life because it gives people someone to trust, talk to, and share experiences with. Having good friends can make difficult situations easier and happy moments more meaningful. However, a strong friendship does not happen automatically. It takes effort, understanding, and honesty from both people to build a relationship that can last for many years.

One of the most important qualities of a good friendship is trust. Friends should feel comfortable sharing their thoughts and problems without worrying that their personal information will be used against them. Being honest with each other is also important, even when the truth is difficult to hear. A real friend does not always agree with everything but can offer advice in a respectful way. In addition, good friends support each other's goals instead of competing with or bringing each other down.

Another important part of friendship is understanding. Every person has different opinions, personalities, and priorities. Because of these differences, misunderstandings and disagreements are sometimes unavoidable. Instead of immediately ending the relationship, friends should learn to listen and communicate calmly. Apologizing when necessary and being willing to forgive can prevent small problems from becoming serious conflicts.

Keeping a friendship strong over time also requires consistent effort. As people grow older, their responsibilities may change. College students, for example, may become busy with schoolwork, family matters, or part-time jobs. Because of this, friends might not see each other as often as they used to. Still, simple actions such as sending a message, checking on each other, or making time to meet can help maintain the connection. Friendship does not require constant communication, but both people should make an effort to show that they still care.

Respecting personal boundaries is equally important. Friends should understand that everyone needs time for themselves and other relationships. Being a good friend means offering support without becoming controlling or demanding.

In the end, a good friendship is built on trust, respect, honesty, and mutual support. Keeping it strong requires patience and effort, especially as life becomes more complicated. When both people are willing to communicate, accept their differences, and remain supportive through changing circumstances, friendship can become one of the most valuable relationships in their lives.

### p12

Learning to manage money at an early age is important because the financial decisions young adults make today can affect their future. Many students begin handling their own allowances, paying for personal expenses, or earning money through part-time work. Although these responsibilities may seem small, they provide opportunities to develop habits that can help them become financially independent later in life.

One major benefit of managing money early is learning how to budget. Young adults often have limited funds, so they need to decide which expenses are necessary and which purchases can wait. For example, a college student may need to divide an allowance among transportation, meals, school supplies, and personal activities. Without a budget, it is easy to spend too much on unnecessary items and run out of money before the next allowance arrives. By tracking expenses and setting spending limits, students can make better financial decisions.

Saving money is another important habit. Even setting aside a small amount regularly can help young adults prepare for unexpected expenses. A student might need money for a sudden school requirement, a medical appointment, or an urgent repair. Having savings can reduce stress and prevent the need to borrow money. Saving early also teaches patience because it encourages people to prioritize long-term goals over immediate wants.

Financial management also helps young adults understand the risks of debt. Credit cards, online loans, and installment payments can make purchases appear more affordable than they actually are. However, borrowing without understanding interest rates and repayment conditions can lead to financial problems. Learning to compare options, read payment terms, and borrow responsibly can help prevent unnecessary debt.

Furthermore, good money management encourages independence and responsibility. Young adults who understand their finances are better prepared to make decisions about education, employment, housing, and future investments. They can also recognize the importance of distinguishing between needs and wants instead of making purchases based only on emotions or social pressure.

Of course, managing money does not mean avoiding every enjoyable purchase. It means finding a reasonable balance between spending, saving, and meeting responsibilities. Financial plans should also be realistic because students have different income levels and family situations.

Overall, learning to manage money early gives young adults practical skills that extend beyond their student years. By developing budgeting, saving, and responsible spending habits, they can reduce financial stress and prepare themselves for a more stable future.

Volunteering is a meaningful activity that allows people to help others without expecting payment in return. It can involve cleaning public spaces, assisting children with their studies, distributing food, or participating in community programs. Although volunteering requires time and effort, its benefits extend beyond the people receiving assistance. It can also help volunteers develop valuable skills, build relationships, and gain a better understanding of the needs of their communities.

For the community, volunteers provide additional support that can help address local problems. Some organizations do not have enough workers or financial resources to meet every need. Volunteers can help organize donation drives, assist during emergencies, and support activities for children and older people. Their contributions may seem small individually, but collective efforts can create meaningful improvements. For example, a group of students cleaning a public area can make the surroundings more pleasant while encouraging residents to take better care of their environment.

Volunteering can also strengthen relationships within a community. When people work together toward a shared goal, they have opportunities to communicate, cooperate, and understand one another. These experiences can encourage unity among individuals from different backgrounds. Community members may become more willing to help each other when they see others contributing their time and effort. As a result, volunteering can build a stronger sense of responsibility and belonging.

The benefits are not limited to the people receiving help. Volunteers can also gain personal and professional experience. Students who participate in community activities may improve their communication, teamwork, leadership, and problem-solving skills. These abilities can be useful in academic projects and future employment. Volunteering also exposes individuals to situations they might not normally encounter, helping them understand social issues such as poverty, educational inequality, and environmental concerns.

Another important benefit is personal growth. Helping others can give volunteers a sense of purpose and accomplishment. It may encourage them to become more patient, compassionate, and appreciative of the opportunities they have. However, volunteering should be approached responsibly. People need to choose activities that match their abilities and available time so they can contribute effectively without neglecting their studies, work, or personal responsibilities.

In conclusion, volunteering creates benefits for both communities and the individuals who participate. Communities receive practical assistance and stronger social connections, while volunteers gain experience, develop important skills, and learn to understand other people's circumstances. Through consistent and meaningful participation, volunteering can become a valuable way to make a positive difference while improving oneself.

Even though people can now access information through smartphones, computers, and social media, reading remains an important skill in the digital age. Technology has made information easier to obtain, but it has also increased the amount of content people encounter every day. Reading helps individuals understand information more deeply, develop their thinking skills, and communicate more effectively. For students in particular, it remains essential for academic success and personal development.

One reason reading is still important is that it improves vocabulary and communication. When people read books, articles, and other written materials, they encounter words and expressions that they may not commonly use in everyday conversations. Over time, this exposure can help them express their ideas more clearly in speaking and writing. Students who regularly read different kinds of materials may also find it easier to understand academic instructions, write essays, and participate in discussions.

Reading also strengthens critical thinking. Instead of simply accepting information, readers can compare arguments, identify evidence, and consider different perspectives. This ability is especially necessary today because false information can spread quickly online. A person who reads carefully and evaluates sources is more likely to recognize misleading claims and distinguish reliable information from unsupported opinions. Reading longer texts can also encourage patience and concentration, which may be difficult to maintain when constantly switching between notifications and short online posts.

Another benefit is that reading provides opportunities for learning beyond the classroom. Books and reliable online articles allow people to explore subjects that interest them, including science, history, technology, and personal development. Students do not have to depend entirely on their teachers to gain knowledge because they can study additional materials independently. Reading fiction also has value because it introduces readers to different experiences and emotions, helping them understand people whose lives may differ from their own.

However, the digital age has changed how people read. Many individuals prefer short posts and summaries because they are convenient. Although these formats can be useful, relying on them exclusively may limit opportunities to develop deeper understanding. Digital reading can also involve distractions, particularly when readers frequently switch between applications.

To make reading more effective, students can set aside a specific time each day, choose materials that interest them, and reduce distractions while studying. Printed books and digital resources can both be useful, depending on personal preferences and accessibility.

Ultimately, reading remains valuable because it develops knowledge, concentration, communication, and critical thinking. Technology may change the way people access written information, but it does not remove the need to understand and evaluate what they read.

### p15

Managing time effectively is an important skill for students because college life often involves several responsibilities at once. Students may need to attend classes, complete assignments, prepare for examinations, participate in group projects, and handle personal matters. Without a clear plan, these responsibilities can become overwhelming and lead to rushed work or unnecessary stress. Fortunately, students can improve their time management by developing practical habits and using their time more intentionally.

One useful strategy is creating a realistic schedule. At the beginning of each week, students can list their deadlines, class hours, meetings, and other important activities. This allows them to see which days will be especially busy and prepare in advance. A planner, calendar application, or simple notebook can help organize these responsibilities. However, the schedule should remain flexible enough to accommodate unexpected tasks and changes.

Another effective approach is prioritizing activities according to their importance and urgency. Not every task deserves the same amount of attention. An assignment due tomorrow may require immediate action, while a project due next month can be divided into smaller tasks completed over several days. By identifying priorities, students can avoid spending too much time on minor activities while neglecting important academic requirements.

Breaking large assignments into smaller steps is also helpful. For example, instead of trying to finish an entire research paper in one sitting, a student can schedule separate periods for selecting a topic, gathering sources, outlining ideas, writing sections, and proofreading. This method makes demanding tasks feel more manageable and reduces the temptation to procrastinate. Starting early also provides enough time to correct mistakes before submission.

Students should also learn to manage distractions. Social media, mobile games, and unnecessary notifications can consume time without being noticed. Turning off notifications, placing a phone away from the study area, or using a timer during study sessions can improve concentration. Taking short breaks between focused periods may also help students maintain their energy without becoming mentally exhausted.

Finally, effective time management requires a healthy balance between academic work and personal well-being. Students should allow sufficient time for sleep, meals, exercise, relaxation, and relationships. Filling every available hour with tasks may appear productive, but it can eventually lead to burnout. Reviewing a weekly schedule can help identify habits that waste time and areas that need improvement.

In conclusion, students can manage their time better by planning ahead, setting priorities, dividing large tasks, limiting distractions, and maintaining a balanced routine. These practices require consistency, but they can make academic responsibilities easier to handle. More importantly, learning to manage time during college prepares students for the demands of employment and adult life.
